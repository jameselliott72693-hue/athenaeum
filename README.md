# The Athenaeum

Seat a table, paste a subject, and read what the chairs can actually support from their books.

The shelf lives in `data/`: one file for the tables, and one file per thinker. Choose a table first. A sitting stays inside that tradition. The present shelf is still there. Three political tables sit beside it, and so do a classical Christian table, an ancient philosophy table, and a Victorian critics table. Three more are single traditions as well: a classical Chinese table with the Dhammapada, an Enlightenment table, and an American founding table. A roster is the `thinkers` list on that table in `data/tables.json`, and the page seats from that list. A new chair also needs a file in `data/thinkers/` and an id in the top-level `thinkers` list.

A line in quotation marks is taken from a public-domain text. Every other card is a summary. Chairs with nothing relevant sit out. A later chair speaks only from a card, or a searched passage, that shares a topic or a term with what was already said. A searched passage is quoted whole, marked “from the full text,” and cited to its book and place. Curated cards win a tie. Brass notes are the room’s framing, not a quotation. Sound is on until you mute it. Pause, step, and speed stay in this browser. A follow-up can go to the whole table or to one chair. Bookmarks, seatings, and full sittings are kept in this browser and can be exported as JSON. A sitting can also be shared as a link in the address bar. If the link would be too long, the page says so and leaves the text and PDF downloads in place. Print includes the citation links. Search the books looks across the current table, or every table. A search result is not a citation until a chair speaks it. Compare asks the same subject at two tables’ usual chairs and lists only the topics and lines those chairs cited.

Thinkers whose books are still in copyright stay on their cards. The list is in `data/books/manifest.json`. To rebuild the corpus and the search index from the raw files named in that script:

```
node scripts/build-books.js
```

The script reads plain text from `BOOK_RAW` (default `/tmp/pg`), strips the Project Gutenberg boilerplate, writes cleaned passages to `data/books/text/`, and writes one BM25 index per thinker to `data/books/index/`. The page loads an index only when a search or a sitting needs that thinker. `BOOK_ONLY=hume,kant` rebuilds just those thinkers and leaves the other index files in place.

The new tables stay inside one tradition each. A few limits are on purpose. Mencius is Book I only: Wilson's anthology marks the other books omitted or does not reach them before the Shi King. Voltaire is Fleming's 1901 dictionary, volume 1, A through Apparition. Hamilton and Madison are only the Federalist papers this Gutenberg text labels with that one name; the three papers labeled "MADISON, with HAMILTON" are in neither index. Washington is the Farewell Address, not the whole volume of messages. Zhuangzi stops before Giles's chapter XXXIII and the subject index. On a narrow screen the list of tables is one column, and the fields stay at a size you can type in.

Jewish thought and the Reformation were looked through again, and they are still not tables. A new table needs six authors, each with a public-domain English book of his own, and a quoted line has to be that book's words. Friedländer's 1910 Guide (Gutenberg 73584) is Maimonides. Whiston's Josephus is a history beside that, not a second philosopher's book. Philo on Gutenberg is Norman Bentwich's biography (14657), not Philo's works. Halevi's diwan there is in German (61327), and the Kuzari appears only as excerpts in Halper's anthology (61386). Husik's history (27821) retells Saadia, Bahya, and the others; it is not their books. Spinoza is not seated to fill a gap. On the Reformation side, Allen's 1813 Institutes (45001) is Calvin, and Luther has the 1915 Philadelphia Works (31604) and the 1921 Bente and Dau Large Catechism (1722). That is two authors with their own books. Hooker's Laws was not found as its own ebook; 13139 is Walton's life of him. Tyndale's Obedience was not found as its own ebook; 70856 is a biography. Knox's History (48250) is Cuthbert Lennox's 1905 revision, marked a twentieth-century edition. Melanchthon's Augsburg Confession (275) names no translator and no year of the English, so it was left off. Three chairs would not make the table.

Open the folder through a local server, or through the hosted site. A file opened directly cannot load the shelf.
